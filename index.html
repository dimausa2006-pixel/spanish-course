    All 42 weeks

    Each week has one topic, 70 words (10 a day), five phrases (one per weekday) and one speaking task for the weekend. Tap a date to open that day's lesson. Tap a word to show or hide its English.

    The list uses the Spanish of Spain: móvil, ordenador, coche, zumo, patata. If you chose Latin America, a few dozen everyday words differ (celular, computadora, carro, jugo, papa). Your tutor will point them out, and week 39 is slang from Spain only. If a word is useless for your life, swap it for one you met while listening.

    Put the words in Anki

    This page shows you the words. Anki makes you remember them, by bringing each one back just before you would forget it. Set it up once.

    1. Install Anki. It's free on a computer and on Android (AnkiDroid). The iPhone app is paid.
    2. Press a copy button above, paste into Notepad, and save the file as spanish.txt with UTF-8 encoding.
    3. In Anki choose File, then Import, and pick the file. Set the separator to Tab. Field 1 is the front, field 2 the back, field 3 the tags (the week number).
    4. For sound, open Tools, Manage Note Types, Basic, Cards, and add {{tts es_ES:Front}} to the front template. Anki then reads each word with your device's Spanish voice.
    5. In the deck options set new cards to 10 a day. Keep the cards in the order of the file.

    Spanish goes on the front because your goal is understanding. Before you flip a card, say the meaning and one short sentence with the word.

    The weekday 30 minutes

    Words, 10 min
    Today's 10 words from this page, read out loud, then tested with the English hidden. After that, your Anki reviews. If reviews start taking more than 10 minutes, set new cards to 5 a day for a week.
    Listening, 15 min
    The material for your current phase. No pausing to look things up. If you understand less than about 70%, pick something easier.
    Speaking, 5 min
    The phrase of the day, copied from a real speaker and then used in sentences of your own. It feels silly for a week and then it doesn't.

    On a bad day, do only the words. Missing listening for a day costs little. Letting Anki reviews pile up for a week is what makes people quit.

    What the time buys you

    Your schedule adds up to about 5.5 hours a week, so roughly 230 hours by September. Schools usually quote about 375 hours of study for B1 and about 550 for B2. Those figures cover reading and writing too, which you are mostly skipping, so the gap is smaller than it looks.

    On the study time alone you end up around the A2 to B1 border. If you also listen to Spanish for 30 minutes a day while you commute, cook or walk, you reach a strong B1 in understanding and speaking. B2 in listening is possible if that extra listening grows to an hour a day. B2 in speaking by September is unlikely on this budget.

    I assumed you are starting from zero. If you already know some Spanish, read the "move on when" line of each phase and start at the first one you can't pass yet. Use the Next day button to jump ahead in the word list.

    The five phases

    Each one ends with a test you can do at home. Pass it and move on to the next phase's listening, even if the calendar says you have a week left. Fail it and stay one more week. The word list keeps going either way.

    1. Sonidos sounds

    Weeks 1 to 6 · 16 Nov to 27 Dec
    Listen
    Language Transfer, Complete Spanish: one or two tracks a day. It's free, audio only, and makes you build sentences out loud from the first lesson. Add Dreaming Spanish "superbeginner" videos at the weekend.
    Speak
    Answer Language Transfer out loud before the student does. Learn to introduce yourself: name, where you live, your job, what you like.
    Words
    Weeks 1 to 6 of the list: greetings, family, core verbs, food, home, time. Total by the end: 420.
    Grammar
    Only what Language Transfer gives you. Present tense of ser, estar, tener, ir, querer, poder, and how to ask a question.
    Move on when
    You can talk about yourself for one minute without notes, and follow a superbeginner video with no subtitles.

    2. Frases sentences

    Weeks 7 to 14 · 28 Dec to 21 Feb
    Listen
    Finish Language Transfer (90 tracks in all). Then Dreaming Spanish "beginner" videos, and the series Extra en español on YouTube, a sitcom made for learners.
    Speak
    Your first real conversations. Book 30 minutes every Saturday on italki or Preply and tell the tutor you want talking only, no textbook. Send them the week's speaking task beforehand. A free exchange on Tandem or HelloTalk also works, but it's less reliable.
    Words
    Weeks 7 to 14: routine, the city, shopping, work, health, describing people, travel, free time. Total: 980.
    Grammar
    Voy a + verb for the future. The past tense of the 20 verbs you use most, learned as whole phrases: fui, comí, hice, tuve.
    Move on when
    You can hold 15 minutes with a patient tutor about your day, your weekend and your plans, and you follow about 80% of a beginner video.

    3. Historias stories

    Weeks 15 to 24 · 22 Feb to 2 May
    Listen
    Dreaming Spanish "intermediate". Podcasts made for learners: Español con Juan, Chill Spanish Listening Practice, Hoy Hablamos. This is the phase where the dead-time listening starts to pay, because podcasts need no screen.
    Speak
    Tell stories in the past: what happened at work, a trip, a film plot. Ask your tutor to correct only the mistakes that made you hard to understand. If money allows, add a second 30 minute session midweek in place of that day's listening.
    Words
    Weeks 15 to 24: storytelling, childhood, cooking, nature, technology, relationships, clothes, repairs, sport, films. Total: 1,680.
    Grammar
    The two past tenses, picked up from stories: estaba for the background, fui for the event. Small words like lo, la, le, by ear.
    Move on when
    You can tell a 3 minute story about last weekend, keep a 30 minute conversation going, and follow a learner podcast while walking.

    4. Opiniones opinions

    Weeks 25 to 34 · 3 May to 11 Jul
    Listen
    Real speech at real speed. Easy Spanish street interviews on YouTube, a series you'd watch anyway with Spanish subtitles on, and Dreaming Spanish "advanced".
    Speak
    Agree, disagree, explain why. Give your tutor the week's topic before each session and argue a side. Sessions go up to 45 minutes.
    Words
    Weeks 25 to 34: opinions, news, working life, money, education, the environment, lifestyle, culture, emotions, and the connectors that make you sound fluent. Total: 2,380.
    Grammar
    The subjunctive as fixed phrases you memorise whole: quiero que vengas, espero que sí, cuando llegue. And me gustaría for polite requests.
    Move on when
    You can defend an opinion for 5 minutes, and you follow an Easy Spanish interview with Spanish subtitles without stopping.

    5. Sin subtítulos no subtitles

    Weeks 35 to 42 · 12 Jul to 5 Sep
    Listen
    Podcasts made for native speakers, on topics you already know well: Radio Ambulante for stories, or anything about your own job or hobby. Series with the subtitles off.
    Speak
    Two conversations a week if you can. Do the speaking tasks from a DELE B1 or SIELE sample exam with your tutor, timed. Find your five most repeated mistakes and work on just those.
    Words
    Weeks 35 to 42: politics, science, media, travel problems, slang, abstract words, advanced verbs, idioms. Total: 2,940.
    Grammar
    "If" sentences: si tengo tiempo, voy and si tuviera tiempo, iría. Retelling what someone said.
    Finish line
    You pass the listening and speaking parts of a sample B1 exam, hold a 45 minute conversation that wanders across topics, and get the gist of a native podcast on a familiar subject.

    Why this method

    Testing yourself
    Pulling a word out of memory, with gaps that grow each time, works better than reading it again. In a long study of Spanish vocabulary, people who left 30 days between sessions remembered about twice as much 8 years later as people who left one day. That is what Anki does, and why this page has a button that hides the English. Review of the research
    Easy listening
    Most of your hours go to Spanish you can mostly follow. Dreaming Spanish recommends at least 30 minutes a day of it for a language close to one you already speak. Their FAQ
    Shadowing
    Repeating a speaker's words right after them, copying the rhythm. A 2025 review of the studies found it helps learners sound clearer and easier to understand. The review
    About 3,000 words
    The 1,000 most common words cover around 80% of everyday language, and about 3,000 cover around 95% of ordinary text. This list has 2,940, grouped by topic. It leans on common words but it isn't a strict frequency list. Summary of the studies

    Four rules

    • Leave the grammar book closed. When a pattern keeps confusing you, look up that one pattern for five minutes and go back to listening.
    • Easy and a lot beats hard and a little. If a video makes you tired after three minutes, it's too hard for now.
    • Speak before you feel ready. Week 7 will feel too early for a tutor. Book it anyway.
    • Keep the Sunday recordings. In March, play the one from December.
